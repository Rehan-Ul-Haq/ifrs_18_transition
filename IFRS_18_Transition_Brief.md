# IFRS 18 Transition Brief

Nov 22, 2026 · Sohail Bana

## Why this matters now

IFRS 18 *Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements* replaces IAS 1 for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, and it must be applied retrospectively. A calendar-year company therefore has to restate its 2026 comparatives, so the data for the comparative year is being captured right now. Recognition and measurement do not change, but the face of the income statement, its subtotals and the notes do, and so do the numbers investors and lenders will quote.

## Key changes

| Area | What IFRS 18 requires | Practical effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Income statement categories | Every income and expense item goes into one of five categories: operating, investing, financing, income taxes, discontinued operations. Operating is the residual category. | Each GL account needs a category mapping. Banks, insurers and investment entities classify some investing and financing items as operating because of their main business activities. |
| Required subtotals | Two new mandatory subtotals: operating profit, and profit before financing and income taxes. | Operating profit becomes comparable across companies and may differ from today's "EBIT" or "operating result". |
| Management-defined performance measures (MPMs) | Subtotals of income and expenses used in public communications (such as adjusted EBITDA) must be disclosed in one audited note, reconciled to the nearest IFRS subtotal, with the tax and non-controlling interest effect of each adjustment. | Investor presentations, MD&A and press releases now feed the audited statements. |
| Aggregation and disaggregation | Items are grouped by shared characteristics and split when material; vague "other" labels are restricted. Expenses presented by function require a note disclosing depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, impairment and inventory write-downs. | Expect more line items and new nature-of-expense data collection. |
| Cash flow statement (IAS 7 amendments) | Operating profit is the starting point for the indirect method. Most companies lose the policy choice for interest and dividends: interest and dividends received go to investing, and interest and dividends paid go to financing. | Cash flow classifications and covenant definitions may shift. |

## Transition implications

- **Restated comparatives.** The 2027 statements present 2026 on the IFRS 18 basis, so 2026 data must be categorised at account level, ideally as each 2026 month closes.
- **Reconciliation disclosure.** For the comparative period, each income statement line item is reconciled from the old IAS 1 presentation to the restated IFRS 18 presentation.
- **Interim reports.** The first condensed interim report in 2027 must already use the new categories and required subtotals, and carries the same reconciliation.
- **Beyond the accounts.** KPIs, debt covenants, remuneration targets, analyst guidance and tax reporting that rely on "operating profit" or adjusted measures may need redefinition.
- **Early adoption** is permitted, and local endorsement dates (for example in Pakistan through ICAP and SECP) should be confirmed for each jurisdiction.

## Practical roadmap

1. **Assess impact (now).** Map the current income statement, notes and public KPIs against IFRS 18; identify main business activities, existing MPMs and covenants affected.
2. **Design (by December 2026).** Set category mapping for every GL account, choose the expense presentation (nature, function or mixed), draft pro forma 2026 statements and the MPM note.
3. **Build data and systems (Q4 2026 to Q1 2027).** Add category and nature tags to the chart of accounts and consolidation tool, and capture 2026 comparatives in parallel.
4. **Govern and communicate (Q1 2027).** Agree policies with the audit committee and auditors, update controls over MPMs, and brief investors, lenders and analysts on the new subtotals.
5. **Report (2027).** Issue the first IFRS 18 interim report with the reconciliation, then the first annual statements with restated comparatives.
